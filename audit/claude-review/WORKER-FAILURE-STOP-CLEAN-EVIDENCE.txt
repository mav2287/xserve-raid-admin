## Mutation evidence

- All four verifier-valid mutants (prefix, report, shim, tail) pass their negative control on both architectures. `check_transport.py:215,228-234` guards each substitution and checks the original bytes before patching (`01ce`→`0133` for shim, `ffffff1b` for tail). `:206` requires the unmodified helper to compile to the candidate's exact bytes.
- **Limitation:** the shim/tail `generated_jar_sha256` values differ between architectures. That's because `writestr` with a plain entry name (`:235`) stamps the current time. Don't treat those hashes as reproducible pins; the byte-edit guards are the real evidence. The source-mutant hashes do match across architectures.

## Preserved semantics (memory seams only)

- Faulted operations keep their original result codes and exception identity: prefix -103, shim -102, generic -102 and the before-request fault -102.
- Original vs candidate: the original continued (`queued_restart=sent`, 2 attempts); the candidate stops before the callback with the restart blocked.

## Unresolved gaps

- **Hardware:** x64 ran under Rosetta, not physical Intel. No release signing or notarization, no real TCP, socket timing or controller operations, and no firmware preflight or transmission binding.
- **Concurrency and liveness:** not qualified:
  - interrupted synchronous waits
  - TYPE_CONNECT completion
  - the post/exit race
  - the manager/queue lock-order deadlock
  - a prefix logger throw at PC 374 killing an already-stopped worker
  - callback or message-getter failures killing workers ("callback-drain-unqualified")
  - failed-stop and subclass paths at runtime
- **GUI and agent:** whole RaidSystemAgent termination and GUI recovery are not measured. The permanent session stop (including a healthy controller hitting the legacy -103 path) is an accepted availability cost. HTTP is still plaintext. A -102 error never proves a mutation wasn't applied.
